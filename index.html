About Me

Hello! I'm Sharon McDermott, born and raised in Los Angeles—specifically, the intersection of Sepulveda and Venice, where, for a decade, the world began and ended outside a pink apartment building and its stubborn patch of grass. I'm the youngest in my family, a position that came with a particular image: delicate, breakable, and needing protection. I didn't love that narrative. So, I rewrote it. Tae Kwon Do became my plot twist—a way to show the world (and myself) that I could hold my own. Somewhere between sparring practice and broken boards, I discovered something else: I liked surprising people. Especially when that person was me.Los Angeles raised me on art, cinema, and history. It's why I draw, can't resist a museum, and gravitate to films where nothing explodes, but everything matters. Classics, romance, comedy; films that linger on the small, honest moments of being human.At ten, my world stretched 285 miles east to Las Vegas, trading palm trees for neon lights that mimicked the absent stars. The desert had fewer art-house theaters, but it was decorated in many personalities. I became an observer, a listener, a collector of stories that weren't mine but felt like they could be. I never used to think much about my own story. I was more interested in writing characters I'd never met but somehow understood. My closet still holds notebooks of secret worlds and nearly-whispered lives that I had written.Psychology and philosophy slipped into my life like they’d always belonged there; like a newly discovered book with an already cracked spine and bent pages. I devoured books and never quite recovered from Frankenstein —a story that feels like a love letter to anyone who's ever felt "other"—and Aristotle's Physics, which I didn't expect to love but did because it made me question everything and stirred a kind of curiosity I hadn’t realized was there.Eventually, I pursued a B.S. in Psychology at UNLV—partly to understand people a little bit better, mainly to help them feel understood in return. Research, as it turned out, wasn't dry and clinical. It was alive. I became a research coordinator and realized I loved turning curiosity into data and data into meaning. That curiosity pushed me to earn an M.S. in Data Science—because I didn't only want to ask questions but find answers in the details. Now, I'm drawn to a career where empathy meets inquiry—where storytelling meets structure.

Contact

Contact me if you want someone who can dive into the chaos of numbers and come back with a story that actually means something, that’s my sweet spot. I’ll bring the data, the curiosity, and the kind of clarity that makes the insight impossible to forget.
